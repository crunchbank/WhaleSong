Surface and blow

The played part of the bar is the sea; its top edge is the water. As the whale comes up, the water bulges, then sends ripples out both ways. They behave like real water: longer waves run ahead, shorter ones trail behind, and each crest grows and fades as it passes through the group. The head breaks the surface first, the whale levels out, and it blows a single bushy spout. It rests and bobs, then sinks back. The animation loops every 13 seconds.

The beats

  1. 0.7 s: the water stirs and bulges, and a first, gentle ripple sets off.
  2. 1.45 s: the head breaks the surface nose-up, climbing along its path, and the main ripple goes out.
  3. 2.2 s: it blows. The spray hangs where it was blown and drifts downwind as the whale swims on.
  4. 2.5–6.3 s: it levels out and bobs gently; each bob sends out a small ripple.
  5. 6.3–10 s: it sinks slowly away nose-down, lingering near the top, tipping about its tail so the tail stays under, with a long, gentle swell, and the sea settles. It swims forward at one steady speed the whole time.
